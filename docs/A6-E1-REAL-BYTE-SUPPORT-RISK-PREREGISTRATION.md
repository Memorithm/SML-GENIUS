## End-to-end question, not another synthetic-only escalation

**Can a bank of genuinely TRAIN-learned native ANF Boolean gates, with uncertainty-aware abstention and an explicit strong-baseline fallback, outperform a simple empirical n-gram on unseen real-language observations?** This first bridge uses the *binary next-space event* `label = (next_utf8_byte == 0x20)`, because it admits an actual 16-bit two-byte Boolean input, learned `WideAnfGate` experts and calibrated binary scoring in an auditable CPU test. It is a **real supervised text subtask**, NOT a next-byte generative language model; success on it cannot satisfy SML-2 on its own. The separate full-next-byte decision remains the existing A5 benchmark, whose strongest historical n-gram4 measured 2.019 bits/byte, versus 2.474 for the best A5 ANF configuration. Those historical figures may NOT be mixed numerically with next-space binary bits/event.

Data: public-domain Jules Verne *Le tour du monde en quatre-vingts jours*, Project Gutenberg eBook #800, through existing `scripts/fetch-char-corpus.sh` with SHA256 `b51f36fb2baf0847fd3632fbd6ff939b8858abc1d728b5f86c94d99e64766a74`. Fail closed if download or hash verification fails, rather than silently substituting synthetic examples. Exact 80%/10%/10% contiguous split and 64 independent chunk lanes, as in A5; first two context bytes per chunk are not scored in this subtask. No future byte crosses a chunk boundary into a feature. TEST observations are not generated or read by the learner, the support discovery or the threshold chooser. No test-driven retraining.

## Predeclared learnable bank and matched controls

Physical Boolean input dimension `N=16`: the eight bits of the preceding two real UTF-8 bytes, with a documented consistent bit-order. Four *different TRAIN-learned* native `WideAnfGate` candidates are fitted with context fan-in `k=2,3,4,5`. Their support positions are selected by a deterministic greedy training-only leave-one-out Krichevsky–Trofimov **binary** log-loss, with ties resolved by lowest physical bit index. All 16 Boolean coordinates compete; there is no supplied hidden true teacher or predeclared privileged role. Their complete Boolean majority truth tables are learned from TRAIN counts and converted exactly to GF(2) ANF using the Möbius transform (a separately tested equality against the truth table). The bank is passed to existing `PassiveCommittee` with prior `[4,2,2,1]/9`, assumed label noise `1/16` and posterior updated from TRAIN only. Candidate posterior confidence is not calibrated by assertion; candidate-generated hard probabilities 0/1 are clipped to `[10^-6,1-10^-6]` **only** for numerical log-loss reporting. Report any collapse to confidence 1.0.

Matched references use **the identical original training/validation/test bytes**: native `char_baselines::NGramModel` order2 and order4, whose binary probabilities are `P(next_byte == 0x20)` from each normalized byte distribution. Preserve the n-grams' original Witten–Bell smoothing and their full-byte training data; report the number of stored counts and timing, do not reinterpret them as ANF parameters. Also report the always-majority-on-TRAIN event predictor. The order4 n-gram is the *hard end-to-end reference* for the binary task, not the historical 2.019 full-byte loss.

## Support, coverage, Hamming and mathematical conditions

Instead of enumerating `C(N,t)` subsets, derive the **sorted union of the four learned candidate input-position supports** (max 5 on this nested greedy panel) from the actual public `WideAnfGate::input_positions()` API; no hidden generator/teacher support may be injected. Create binary strength-`t=2,3` pattern coverage on precisely this union; track each `t`-subset and all its `2^t` assignments. The explicit expected space is `C(s,t)` tuple records plus `2^t` mask bits per record, with `s=|union_support|`, not `N^t`; both `max_support=16` and `max_tuples=1024` must be enforced before mutation. Report memory as logical payload vs allocation/RSS, and report all false abstentions. A partial or incomplete support list is never called whole-model certification.

Complement this with an **exact full-16-bit-input** nearest-TRAIN Hamming distance. Store seen 16-bit configurations then compute all 65,536 minimal distances by a deterministic 16-dimensional cube multi-source BFS at seal time. Thus query is constant bounded 16-bit lookup, with no arbitrary global `N` scaling claim. All training rows are observed before the guard is sealed; any later addition is forbidden. Source-history mismatch must fail closed.

*Degree is an explicit assumption, not an observed law of natural text.* For a set of `s` coordinates, the hypothetical ANF polynomial class of degree `<=d` has `M(s,d)=sum_{i=0}^d C(s,i)` candidate monomial coefficients. Expose the TRAIN-only GF(2) **design-matrix rank** for `d=1,2`, with predeclared size budget `M<=64`. A full-rank design plus consistent noiseless labels and the **assumption that the unknown teacher actually belongs to this degree-bounded, support-bounded grammar** gives algebraic identifiability. Pairwise/three-way pattern coverage alone does NOT imply full rank or realizability. For the real corpus (noisy/misspecified unknown teacher), NEVER emit a certainty certificate from rank alone.

**Mandatory analytical counterexample unit test:** `TRAIN={000,011,101,110}` on three bits covers all binary patterns on every pair, yet `f(x)=0` and `g(x)=x0 XOR x1 XOR x2` agree on TRAIN and disagree at `001`. Even for `d=1` or `d=2`, pairwise coverage is insufficient to infer the truth. Preserve this negative alongside positive exact-oracle tests.

For actively generated covering arrays in *future intervention-enabled synthetic* experiments, NIST's `O(2^t log n)` scaling refers to **number of rows covering combinatorial input patterns**, with construction- and t-dependent constants. It is NOT the cost of storing/querying every tuple, nor an identifiability theorem, nor a source of real-world counterfactual text labels. Do not feed synthetic covering-array labels as if they were actual future bytes.

## Frozen risk–coverage policy grid, fairness and stop

Return all policies and their absolute coverage/risk, not only a cherry-picked best. Grid includes committee confidence `c in {0.50,0.90}`, support strength `t in {2,3}` and maximum novel-tuple count `m in {0,1}`, full-input Hamming threshold `h in {0,1,2,4}`: **32 combined policies**, plus matched no-guard/standalone support/standalone Hamming controls. The controlling fraction is `accepted / total`; **selective classification error** is `wrong / accepted`, undefined and explicitly marked if accepted=0. Abstentions are neither labeled correct nor removed from the end-to-end score.

The deployment policy emits the ANF-committee prediction ONLY for accepted inputs; otherwise it falls back to the **fixed n-gram4 distribution**. Score **every** TEST observation using binary log-loss (`bits/event`) and classification error, and report accepted-only errors, coverage, fallback fraction, and the n-gram2/n-gram4 unconditional losses. Policies are compared on identical chronological observations; no selective accuracy without its coverage and fallback loss.

Choose one configuration using VAL only: among the 32 combined policies with VAL coverage `>=0.80`, select the minimum **full-coverage fallback log-loss**, ties resolved lexicographically by `(c,t,m,h)`. If no policy meets 80% coverage, mark the model **NOT PROMOTABLE** without using TEST to select. Once frozen, evaluate TEST exactly once. A binary-task **conditional research pass** requires TEST fallback log-loss at least `0.02 bits/event` lower than matched n-gram4, VAL-selected policy TEST coverage `>=0.80`, and no additional process memory over the declared design budget. All three criteria must hold; otherwise **STOP / negative result**, not a reason to silently loosen thresholds or invent more guards. A second independent corpus and a full-next-byte model are required for SML-2 promotion in any case.

The matched n-gram4 baseline alone achieves 100% task predictions and will be recorded even if no ANF policy improves it. Record training/selection/inference wall times and work budgets on the exact hardware; speedups require matched hardware and comparable output work.
